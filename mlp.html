I didn't design mylandscapingproject.ca from a rulebook. I designed it from 25 years of studying design and human behaviour — and since 2015, from running my own design-build firm and rebuilding its site until I understood exactly how a homeowner wants to decide. Then I did the thing most people never do: I went back and named why it worked, principle by principle, and turned it into a system. This is the systematic way my mind works — and this chapter is that method, read backwards off the page that produced it.

So let's take one page — the pricing page — and take it apart.

Budget as a self-selecting door

Instead of hiding pricing, visitors pick their own range and see real projects at that level. It qualifies the lead before the first call — and a firm that shows its ranges signals it isn't desperate for any job.

Transparency · Selective Confidence

Investment, not price

The page never says "cost." Every project reads Investment: $16,375 · Property Value Increase: $25–30k. The scariest number on the page becomes the most reassuring — because the unknown price is the number-one reason a homeowner leaves.

Radical Transparency

Selling the morning coffee

Every project's story leads with the family, not the specs — the unsafe old bridge, the mom who sketched her vision, the coffee by the waterfall. Homeowners decide emotionally and justify rationally: the feeling closes; the scope sheet just confirms it (Kahneman's System 1).

Emotional Sequencing

The role-play Q&A

The FAQ isn't generic — it's the actual doubts clients voiced, answered the way I'd answer them on the phone. Handle the objection before it's raised, and you prove you've heard it a hundred times. Answer honestly first, earn the call (Cialdini's reciprocity).

The Advisor Position
“A well-informed consumer will always make a much more satisfying decision about who they go with.”— my own words, from the walkthrough. The entire page exists to inform.

None of this was luck, and none of it was taste. It was a repeatable set of moves — which is exactly why I could extract it. The method, made repeatable.
